## Intended informal theorem

Write `<a,b,...>` for the additive submonoid of the natural numbers generated
by the displayed integers. Then:

1. `2n ∈ <54,106,107,108>` if and only if `n ∈ <27,53>`.
2. Put `r(n)=(27-(n mod 27)) mod 27`. Then
   `n ∈ <27,53>` if and only if `53*r(n) ≤ n`.
3. Every `n≥1352` belongs to `<27,53>`, while `1351` does not. Thus `1351`
   is the Frobenius number.
4. Exactly `663` integers in `[14,1351]` do not belong to `<27,53>`.

For (1), parity forces the coefficient of the odd generator `107` to be even.
Writing it as `2e`, dividing by two, and replacing `107e` with
`54e+53e` yields the two-generator representation. For (2), `53≡-1`
modulo `27`, so `r(n)` is the least nonnegative compatible coefficient of
`53`.

## Trust boundary

The Lean source contains no `sorry`, `admit`, custom axiom, `unsafe`, or
`native_decide`. It uses no external file, generated proof data, solver,
floating point, randomness, or nonstandard kernel/plugin.

This project does **not** formalize or certify:

- construction of the packing-total transfer automaton;
- the reported `339203` states and `437094` edges;
- SCC or exhaustive simple-cycle enumeration;
- the claim that all closed-walk lengths of the automaton lie in
  `<54,106,107,108>`;
- the graph-coloring/closed-walk equivalence; or
- seven-color impossibility.

Those are explicit external bridges from the graph contribution. Conditional
on its four-generator closed-walk classification, Lean proves all stated
arithmetic consequences without trusting the external implementation.
