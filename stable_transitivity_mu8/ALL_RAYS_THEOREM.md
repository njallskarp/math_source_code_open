# Exact integral growth on every exceptional order-eight ray

## Theorem

Let `T` be any tournament completion of the canonical partial tournament
`G8` defined in `M6_THEOREM.md`.  Equivalently, up to isomorphism, let `T`
be one of the 96 order-eight tournaments with `m(T)=2`.  Then for every
integer `k>=1`,

    m(k T) = ceil(7k/6).                                (1)

Moreover, for each `d=2,3,4,5`, the upper bound at `k=d` has a square-free
profile: there are `3d+2` distinct total orders that predict every arc of
`T` exactly `2d+1` times.

## Universal lower bound

Every total order predicts at most 13 of the 20 arcs of `G8`.  If an
`a`-order stabilizer exists for `kT`, reversing its orders produces
`k+2a` total orders that predict each `T` arc exactly `k+a` times.  Counting
predictions on the `G8` arcs gives

    20(k+a) <= 13(k+2a).

Thus `7k<=6a`, and consequently

    m(kT) >= ceil(7k/6).                                (2)

This argument is independent of the residue certificates.

## The four residue fibers

For every one of the 96 representatives and each `d=2,3,4,5`,
`residue_profiles.txt` gives a set of `3d+2` total orders whose sum in the
28 pair coordinates is

    d x(T) + (d+1) 1.                                  (3)

Equivalently, every `T` arc is predicted `2d+1` times and missed `d+1`
times.  Partition any `2d+1` profile orders into `A`, let `R` be the
remaining `d+1`, and let `B` contain the reversals of the orders in `R`.
Equation (3) then gives

    d x(T) + B = A,

so `m(dT)<=d+1`.  Equation (2) makes this sharp.

The common dual face gives a useful certificate invariant.  If the `G8`
defect of an order is `13` minus its number of predicted `G8` arcs, then the
seven defect layers have sizes

    832, 4192, 9344, 11584, 9344, 4192, 832.

Every sharp residue-`d` profile has total defect exactly `6-d`; this is why
orders of defect greater than `6-d` may be removed from the discovery MILP
without losing a solution.

## Passage to every dilation

Write `k=6q+r` with `0<=r<6`.  The scale-six theorem supplies a profile for
`6qT` with stabilizer size `7q`.  For `r=1`, the independently reviewed
order-eight classification supplies `m(T)=2`.  For `r=2,3,4,5`, use the
profiles above with stabilizer size `r+1`.  Adding profiles gives respectively

    7q, 7q+2, 7q+3, 7q+4, 7q+5, 7q+6,

which is `ceil(7k/6)` in every residue.  Together with (2), this proves (1).

## Algebraic interpretation

Let `S_T` be the affine semigroup generated by the 40,320 linear-ordering
vertices, graded by profile size.  Reversal pairs give the neutral element
`1` in pair coordinates and degree two.  The certificate proves that the
six residue targets

    d x(T) + (d+1) 1,  1<=d<=6,

all lie in `S_T` (the cases `d=1` and `d=6` come from the two cited
dependencies).  Addition by the degree-20 scale-six element fills the whole
ray.  Thus (1) is a residue-module saturation theorem for one exposed
semigroup face, not an extrapolation from a growing numerical census.

## Trust boundary

`verify_residues.py` uses only standard-library integer arithmetic.  It
independently enumerates all 40,320 order masks, verifies the complete dual
defect distribution for each class, checks canonical coverage of all 384
residue profiles, rejects multiplicities, and checks all 10,752 arc-count
equations.  The SciPy/HiGHS MILP generator is outside the correctness
boundary.  The theorem also depends on the independently reviewed
height-1669/1675 fact `m(T)=2`, and on the same-author scale-six theorem at
height 2463.  The latter has not yet received independent mathematical
review, so the combined all-ray theorem remains provisional pending that
audit.
