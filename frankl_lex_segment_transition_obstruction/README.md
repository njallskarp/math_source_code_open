# Frankl lex-segment transition obstruction

This directory gives a compact exact certificate for a boundary phenomenon
around the lexicographic initial segment in the Frankl-complete-family problem.
It concerns the conjecture of Pulaj and Wood in
[Local Configurations in Union-Closed Families](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01331)
(Experimental Mathematics, DOI
[10.1080/10586458.2024.2410964](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2024.2410964)).

## Precise claim

Order the 4-subsets of `[9]` lexicographically and let `A` be the first 13.
Pulaj and Wood already established that `A` is Non-FC; this is the witness for
their lower bound `FC(4,9) >= 14`.  The checker supplies a particularly compact
Poonen dual for that known fact and establishes a new, deliberately limited
transition obstruction:

1. Twelve union-closed, `A`-stable families `B_j` have frequency rows
   `q(B_j)_i = 2 |(B_j)_i| - |B_j|`.  Their nonnegative Farkas multipliers have
   aggregate

   ```text
   (-2, -2, -12, -12, -6, -6, -6, -2, -2).
   ```

   Hence no nonzero nonnegative weight vector can satisfy every inequality
   `q(B_j) . c >= 0`.  Poonen's characterization therefore certifies that `A`
   is Non-FC.

2. For every one of the 113 remaining 4-sets `g`, at least four of these twelve
   witnesses cease to be stable after adjoining `g` to `A`.

3. The canonical one-step repair

   ```text
   B_j^g = B_j union {S union g : S in B_j}
   ```

   also cannot carry this dual across the transition: for every `g`, the
   checker supplies a nonzero nonnegative vector satisfying all twelve repaired
   inequalities.  The 113 cases compress to 30 orbits under the subgroup
   generated by `(1 2)`, `(3 4)`, `(6 7)`, and `(8 9)`.

The last statement is an obstruction to reusing or canonically repairing this
specific dual certificate.  It does **not** prove that `A union {g}` is FC, and
it does not settle the lexicographic initial-segment conjecture.

## Why the repair remains admissible

If `B` is union-closed, then
`B^g = B union {S union g : S in B}` is union-closed: a union of two members is
respectively of the form `S union T`, `(S union T) union g`, or the same latter
form, with `S union T` in `B`.  If `B` is stable under every member of the
union-closure of `A`, then `B^g` is stable under the union-closure of
`A union {g}`, because adjoining old generators commutes with adjoining `g`.
The checker verifies the resulting stability directly for every repaired
family.

## Reproduce

Requires Python 3.10 or later and no third-party packages.

```bash
python3 verify.py
```

Expected output is recorded in `EXPECTED_OUTPUT.txt`.  On Python 3.12.12 the
check takes about five seconds on the discovery machine.

## Encoding and trust boundary

Subsets of `[9]` are 9-bit integers, with bit `i-1` representing element `i`.
Nine witnesses are generated from the standard
`A uplus P([9] - {j})` construction, two have short implication rules, and one
236-member witness is stored explicitly.  The checker verifies union closure,
stability under the full generated closure, all frequency rows, the exact
integer Farkas identity, subgroup coverage, and all 1,356 repaired inequalities.

Z3 4.15.3 and Gurobi 12.0.3 (with lazy Horn cuts) were used only to discover
candidate witnesses and weights.  They are not dependencies and their verdicts
are not trusted by the retained certificate.  The remaining trust boundary is
the stated reduction through Poonen's theorem, the mathematical repair lemma
above, Python's integer/set semantics, and inspection of `verify.py`; there is
no proof-assistant kernel or external data input.
